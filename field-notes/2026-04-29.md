## AI Speeds You Up Into the Wrong Place

AI is a powerful accelerator — but it accelerates you toward *building*, not toward *thinking*. The risk is that you move fast into the wrong direction before you've done the hard work of understanding the process your users actually go through.

That's what happened here. The early hours went into designing an app — menus, meal types, one-off cooking vs. weekly prep. It felt like progress. It looked like progress. But it was building the end product before understanding the entry point.

## The Real Unlock: Pantry Tracking

The core insight today: the most valuable thing Stock Up Dinners can do is not tell people what to cook — it's tracking what they already have.

Once the pantry is tracked:
- Shopping lists write themselves (buy what you're actually missing)
- Recipe recommendations become immediately relevant (use what's already there)
- The product gets smarter the more someone uses it

Everything else — the 14 meal plans, the Costco shopping list lead magnet, the recipe browsing — these are acquisition surfaces. The pantry tracker is the retention engine and the value core.

## Process Before Product

The lead magnet work (Costco staples list + 14 meals) turned out to be generative, not just promotional. Building it forced the question: *after* someone downloads this and cooks a few meals, what does the ongoing relationship with this product look like?

That question led directly to pantry tracking as the answer.

The lesson isn't to skip product thinking — it's to start with the user's ongoing process, not the final feature set. The app design got complicated because it started in the wrong place. Now there's a clear foundation to rebuild from.
