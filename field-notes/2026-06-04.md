Frontier models and the honesty trap. Anyone who has used a top-tier model recently notices it overuses the word "honestly" or "to be honest." The reason it grates: you don't want your LLM to be honest, you want it to be truthful.

Honesty is a moderated stance. When a model says it's being honest, it's giving you an interpretation filtered through a theory of mind — it's treading softly, trying to match its output to what you'll receive well. Honesty can't be challenged on its own terms. A model can be perfectly honest and completely wrong, which produces an output that is useless to you.

Truthfulness is empirical. A truthful model states things plainly and can be challenged on the grounds of its claims. "Is this accurate?" is a tractable question. "Is this honest?" is not. You can argue back and forth over truthfulness; over honesty you really can't.

The practical implication for AI product design: a frontier model optimized for perceived honesty is optimizing for social acceptance, not utility. A model optimized for truthfulness is optimizing for correctness. These are different objectives with different failure modes, and builders should choose deliberately.
