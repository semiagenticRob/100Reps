Spent yesterday at the Englewood Chamber of Commerce for an SBDC event on AI. Walked in without expectations and left with a sharp reminder: meat space and internet space are completely different worlds.

On the internet — looking at what other builders are shipping, the tooling they're using, the projects on [[rep:14]] and beyond — it's easy to feel behind. The pace feels fast, the competition feels dense, and everyone seems to be further along.

In a room of actual business owners and operators? The dynamic flips entirely. It became clear that I was the most experienced person in the room when it came to AI. Not because the bar was low — but because these people are simply not on X or in the builder communities where this stuff is discussed. The hunger to use these tools is real and it's enormous. The distribution gap between "builder Twitter" and Main Street business owners is bigger than I'd appreciated.

The other thing: showing up counts. Just by attending a single event, I connected with four businesses that want to talk. No funnel, no cold email, no ad spend. Just presence.

Lesson candidate: **offline rooms are a distribution unlock that internet-native builders systematically underestimate.**
