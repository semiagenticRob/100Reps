What early and aggressive adopters of AI and AR are finding is counterintuitive: it doesn't reduce the workload of the organization — it drastically grows the organization and the need for expertise.

The explanation is that AI is unlocking an **insight-based economy**, as opposed to a rote or execution-based economy.

White collar work has always followed a predictable arc: humans master a domain of rote work, then abstract it away into software. Think accounting, legal document review, data entry. Each generation of tooling pushes the automation frontier deeper into what used to require human judgment.

AI is now doing something more radical — it's pushing the automation function into the lowest layer of the rote economy entirely, and in doing so, it's enabling what I'm calling the **insight-based AI-driven economy**.

The law firm is the clearest example. Traditionally, a 1st–3rd year associate does document review — a kind of white collar factory work, cutting their teeth on high-volume, low-judgment tasks before climbing to more complex work. AI takes over that function. But this doesn't mean legal problems go unsolved. It means that 1st year associate now trains up on the **meta-problems of law** — the managerial, strategic, inside-space problems — far faster than they otherwise would.

This is a radical differentiator. AI compresses the apprenticeship curve. Individuals skill up to mastery far quicker than they could before.

The takeaway: AI is not eliminating domains. It's **accelerating mastery within them**. The organizations that understand this will grow. The ones waiting for headcount reduction are going to be confused when demand expands instead.
